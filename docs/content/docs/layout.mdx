## What herdr returns

Each herdr tab is a split tree: every split has a direction (`right` or
`down`) and a ratio. herdr stores it across restarts.
`session.snapshot.layouts[]` gives each tab's layout as a flat list:
```json
{"tab_id":"w1:t1","area":{"width":120,"height":40},
 "panes":[{"pane_id":"w1:p1","rect":{…}},{"pane_id":"w1:p3","rect":{…}},{"pane_id":"w1:p2","rect":{…}}],
 "splits":[{"id":"split_0_root","direction":"right","ratio":0.3},
           {"id":"split_1_0","direction":"down","ratio":0.5}]}
```
- A split's `id` encodes its path from the root: `root`, then one digit per
  step (`0` = first child, `1` = second).
- `panes[]` is in depth-first order, first child before second.

## Rebuilding the tree

```
build(path) = split at path exists ? Split(dir, ratio, build(path+0), build(path+1))
                                   : Leaf(next pane from panes[])
```
- About 15 lines. If the leaf count does not equal `panes.count`, the app
  falls back to `layout.export {tab_id}` (the same tree, nested). The order is
  depth-first in herdr's source but not a documented contract, hence the
  check.

## Drawing and resizing

- A tab page is the size of the strip. Each split divides its rectangle by the
  ratio, with a fixed gap between cards. herdr's cell rectangles are not used
  for drawing.
- Each terminal card tells herdr its own size in cells, so the PTY fits the
  card.
- Dragging a divider moves the split in the view at once (8 pt hit area,
  resize cursor). On release the app sends `layout.set_split_ratio`; `path`
  is the split's id read as booleans. The ratio stays within herdr's 0.1–0.9.
- Resizing works on the split tree only. To put a pane elsewhere, move it.

## Zoom is the app's own

- One `zoomed` card per window, in memory.
- herdr's `pane.zoom` is not used: it moves herdr's focus, marks the whole tab
  seen, and makes herdr refuse moves into or out of that tab.
- If a herdr TUI user zoomed a tab, the app ignores that flag and draws the
  full layout. A move into that tab returns `zoomed_tab`, which the app shows.

## PTY size has one owner

- While our `control` stream is attached, herdr locks that PTY to our size
  and ignores herdr TUI layout resizes. After release the PTY keeps our size
  until a herdr TUI lays the tab out again.
- herdr's layout rectangles never show the PTY size; the frame's `width` and
  `height` do.

## Selection and "seen" are the app's own

- The selected machine, workspace, tab and focused card live in memory, plus
  `@SceneStorage` to restore the window. A restore id that is gone falls back
  to herdr's focused workspace.
- **The app never calls herdr's focus methods** (`pane.focus`, `tab.focus`,
  `workspace.focus`, `agent.focus`). Each of them moves every attached herdr
  TUI to that tab and marks the tab seen (herdr source, and seen in our
  tests). A herdr TUI may be open next to the app, and it must not jump.
- So the app tracks "seen" itself, in memory, per agent:
  - The first time the app sees an agent, it records the agent's current
    `completion_seq` as seen, so old finishes do not flood the status area.
  - Viewing the agent's pane records the current value.
  - Simpler choice: finishes that happen while the app is closed are not
    shown.
